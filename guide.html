Survival 101

How to approach your first weeks in 7 Days to Die

Vanilla-only advice for brand-new survivors. No mod jargon — just what to do before the horde arrives, how to loot safely, and where to spend your first skill points.

The mindset: survive the schedule, not the map

7 Days to Die runs on a day counter. Every seventh night is a blood moon — zombies hunt you harder and spawn aggressively. Your entire early game is a race to have walls, a weapon, food, and a bed before day 7, then to improve before day 14, 21, and so on.

Rule of thumb: If you are debating whether to explore or reinforce base on day 6, reinforce. Looting can wait; a failed blood moon cannot.

Your first hour (day 1)

  1. Grab everything on the ground. Stones, plant fibers, feathers, wood — you need them for stone axe, bow, and bandages. Check the resources and tools sections if you are unsure what matters.
  2. Craft a stone axe immediately. It is your wood source and emergency weapon. Unlock via the First Week Miner template on a Strength start. In the catalog, tools and resources show in-game icons after you sync icons from your game install.
  3. Find a building before dusk. Any POI roof beats sleeping in the open. Planted trees and zombies both ruin a naked first night.
  4. Make a bedroll or bed. Your respawn point should be inside your temporary shelter. Losing your backpack across the map is how runs end on day 2.
  5. Spike traps at choke points. One door, one corridor, wood spikes. See traps & defense for early options.

Days 2–7: what actually matters

Food & water

Boiled water (cooking pot on campfire) stops dysentery. Corn bread and stew beat raw meat. Living Off The Land pays off after day 7 if you plant early.

Forge & iron

Mine boulders or surface nodes with a stone axe, smelt in a forge, craft forged iron. Iron tools and weapons are the mid-game gate.

Base shape

Two stories: ground floor traps and storage, upper floor shooting windows. Cobblestone or concrete before wood on horde-facing walls. See structural integrity before you dig out under your base.

Skill points

You earn one point per level. Do not spread across all five attributes — pick a plan on the planner and commit for 10+ levels.

Structural integrity (building blocks)

Bases in 7 Days to Die obey physics. Every block has a Max Load (how much weight each face can hold) and a Mass (how heavy the block is). Think of Max Load as glue — if you hang too much mass off a wall or ceiling, blocks collapse or refuse to place.

Quick formula: Unsupported span per face ≈ round down (Max Load ÷ Mass). Check any block’s tooltip in-game for its exact numbers.

Placement preview colors

  • Yellow — Stable. Brighter yellow means less margin before failure.
  • Red — Placing here would cause a collapse unless you reinforce elsewhere first.
  • Pink — This block would fall immediately; not enough support.

Vertical vs horizontal

  • Vertical stacks are unlimited — you can pile hundreds of blocks on one pillar (until you hit the world height cap). A one-block pillar base is possible but suicidal on horde night.
  • Horizontal spans are limited — each block you extend sideways counts toward the support limit of the block it hangs from.
  • The “magic 8” rule — on a single-block-thick floor or bridge, the 9th block from support will collapse when you step on it (count every block between you and the pillar, including turns).
  • Pillar material matters for attachments — stack the tower in wood if you want, but use iron frames or metal trussing on the segment that must hold heavy side loads.

Base-building mistakes to avoid

  • Undermining your own pillar — digging out the ground under a “green” support column turns the whole stack into unsupported blocks. One wrong mining swing can drop your floor.
  • Upgrading inconsistently — pouring concrete over metal rebar frames temporarily lowers SI to wood-tier stats until the full upgrade chain completes. Use temporary supports when upgrading.
  • Weak variants — some wood shapes (e.g. shingles) have lower Max Load than standard frames. Always read the tooltip before using them structurally.
  • Mixing materials blindly — heavy blocks on a wood anchor fail sooner than the same span in one material. Match your anchor to the heaviest attached mass.

Blood moon survival (day 7, 14, 21…)

On blood moon night, zombies sense you from farther away and spawn to pressure your location. Fighting in the open is a mistake unless you are heavily geared.

  • Stay inside your base — verticality helps (shoot down, they climb slowly).
  • One kill box — funnel zombies through spikes or a single door you can melee.
  • Repair during the day — check trap durability and wall HP after every horde.
  • Shotguns and melee shine here — see the Horde Night Shotgun template if you want a combat focus.
  • Skip the trader run — night looting is for veterans; prep before sunset on day 6.
First blood moon tip: If you only have a bow, fight from a rooftop with a clear line of sight. Do not stand in the street “kiting” — runners will flank you.

POI looting without throwing the run

Points of Interest (houses, stores, factories) are where tiered loot lives — but they are also zombie magnets. Treat every POI as a tactical mission, not a shopping spree.

Before you enter

  • Clear the yard first — stragglers inside will hear you break in.
  • Mark an exit route; never loot the only doorway until you have a hole in the wall or roof escape.
  • Bring food, water, bandages, and spare ammo. Running out mid-POI is how you die tired.

Inside the building

  • Sneak when possible — bonus damage and fewer alerts (Agility / Archery path).
  • Loot the roof last — work downward so you are not trapped upstairs.
  • Smash strategically — Salvage Operations and Lucky Looter reward Perception investment; try the POI Salvager template on the builds page.
  • Leave the vending machine until you have inventory space and a clear exit.

Search the catalog for POI staples: lockpicks, medical, food.

Perk order for beginners

Attributes gate perk levels — you cannot take perk level 3 without attribute level 3. Raise your main attribute first, then fill one combat or utility tree before dabbling elsewhere.

PriorityWhat to buyWhy
1 One main attribute to 3–4 Unlocks tier-2–3 crafts in your chosen tree
2 Pack Mule 2–3 (Fortitude) Almost every build wants carry capacity early
3 Your weapon perk to match what you craft Club / bow / shotgun — commit, do not split points
4 Lucky Looter 1–2 (Perception) Better containers; cheap for any loot-heavy playstyle
5 Healing Factor or Living Off The Land Quality of life once combat basics are online

Avoid maxing five different weapon trees. Pick one ranged or melee line and ride it to steel tier. Use the planner at your real survivor level to see if a build fits — max level 300 gives 303 skill points.
